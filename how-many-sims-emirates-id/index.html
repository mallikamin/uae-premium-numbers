How Many SIM Cards Can One Emirates ID Hold?

There is a cap, it is set per operator rather than across the country, and the published policy deliberately does not name a figure. Here is what is actually written down, and how to check what is already registered against your name.

Short answer

Is there a limit? Yes, but not the single national number people expect. The regulator's policy requires each operator to ensure no consumer is registered for more than the maximum number of SIM cards with that operator, and it says that maximum is determined by the Authority from time to time. The figure is not stated in the policy itself. So the cap is per operator, it can change, and anyone quoting you a hard number without naming a source and a date is guessing.

What the rule actually says

The relevant line in the regulator's mobile registration policy is short. Each operator must ensure that each consumer is not registered for more than the maximum number of SIM cards with that operator, as that maximum is determined by the Authority from time to time.

Three things follow from that one sentence, and all three matter.

  • The cap is per operator, not national. Your e& count and your du count are separate ceilings. There is no single number in the policy covering everything you hold across the country.
  • The figure lives outside the policy. It is set by the Authority and can be revised, which is why the policy text does not pin it down. Any specific number you read online is only as good as its source and its date.
  • The obligation sits on the operator. It is the operator that has to enforce the ceiling at the point of registration, which is why you find out at the counter.

The honest answer to the question in the title is therefore: ask e& for the current figure, or ask the Authority. We are not going to invent a number and put it in a table just because that is what the question wants. What we can tell you precisely is how to see what you already have, which is the part most people actually need.

How to see every number registered in your name, free

You have two routes, and both are free.

1. The regulator's own service

Hesabati shows the mobile and fixed line numbers registered under your Emirates ID number with telecom providers in the UAE. It is free, it is open to anyone holding an Emirates ID, and you reach it by logging in to the Authority's website with UAE Pass. The stated purpose is transparency and preventing misuse of an identity, specifically making sure no SIM is registered under your ID without your knowledge.

2. Your operator, as an obligation

Separately, every operator is required to provide a free, confidential and secure service through which you can determine the details of any subscription contract you are party to, the status of any pending registration or renewal, and the registration details of any SIM registered to you. In practice that is the e& app, or a business centre with your original Emirates ID.

That last row is the one that quietly costs people numbers. A line you have forgotten is still on a registration clock, and the sequence from expiry to the number being reused is fixed and unforgiving.

Lines registered to you, versus lines you use

These are not the same thing and the distinction matters when you hit a ceiling. What counts against you is what is registered to your identity document, regardless of who is walking around with the handset. A line you took out for a family member, a driver or a domestic worker is your registration, your ceiling, and your responsibility when the documents expire.

If that is the situation you are in, two cleaner options exist. Move the line properly into the other person's name, which is a regulated transfer the operator has to allow once five conditions are met. Or, if the lines genuinely belong together as a household, put them on a family arrangement built for multiple lines rather than stacking unrelated registrations on one ID.

Frequently asked questions

How many SIM cards can be registered to one Emirates ID in the UAE?

There is a maximum, but it is set per operator rather than nationally, and the regulator's published policy does not state the figure. The policy requires each operator to ensure a consumer is not registered for more than the maximum number of SIM cards with that operator, as determined by the Authority from time to time. Ask e& for its current figure.

Is the SIM limit per operator or across all UAE operators?

Per operator. The rule is written as a maximum with that operator, so your e& count and your du count are separate ceilings rather than one shared national total.

How can I see how many SIMs are registered in my name?

Two free routes. Hesabati, run by the regulator, shows the mobile and fixed line numbers registered under your Emirates ID with UAE providers, accessed through the Authority's website using UAE Pass. Separately, every operator must provide a free confidential service showing the registration details of any SIM registered to you.

Is Hesabati free, and who can use it?

It is free and it is available to anyone holding an Emirates ID. You log in to the regulator's website with UAE Pass. Its stated purpose includes making sure no SIM is registered under your identity without your knowledge.

What if a SIM is registered under my Emirates ID without my knowledge?

That is the specific problem Hesabati was created to surface. Check the list, then raise anything you do not recognise with the operator it sits with. Operators are also required to keep registration information secure and confidential.

Do lines I bought for family members count against my limit?

Yes. What counts is what is registered to your identity document, not who uses the handset. If a line really belongs to someone else, transferring it into their name is a regulated process the operator has to allow once the conditions are met.

Can I get a second Etisalat line on the same Emirates ID?

Yes, provided you are inside your operator's ceiling. It is an ordinary new subscription needing your original Emirates ID plus one supporting document, and it carries the same free premium number tier as any other postpaid plan.

What happens to a second line I stop using?

It stays on its registration clock. When the registration expires the operator suspends the line, and three months after suspension it can deactivate it and reuse the number. Forgotten second lines are the most common way people lose a number.
